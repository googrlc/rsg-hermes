# Commercial Auto Reference

The reference guide for commercial auto quoting and service.
Complements `commercial-risk-intake` (which extracts data from
submissions) with the checklists, rules, and templates Gretchen uses.

## When to use

- "What do I need for a commercial auto quote?"
- "What are the radius rules?"
- "How do I look up a DOT or MC number?"
- "What is hired and non-owned auto?"
- "What filings do we need?"

## Commercial auto intake checklist

1. Business legal name and DBA
2. FEIN
3. Business address (garaging address for vehicles)
4. Entity type (LLC, Corp, Sole Prop, Partnership)
5. Years in business
6. Nature of operations (what do they haul? what do they do?)
7. Radius of operations (local, intermediate, long haul)
8. States operated in
9. Driver schedule (see template)
10. Vehicle schedule (see template)
11. Current carrier
12. Current premium
13. Expiration date
14. Loss runs (3-5 years)
15. DOT number (if applicable)
16. MC number (if interstate)
17. MVRs for all drivers
18. Desired coverages (liability limit, comp, coll, cargo, etc.)
19. Any filings required (Form E, Form H, etc.)

## Progressive commercial auto appetite

- Strong appetite for local and intermediate radius.
- Long haul: more restrictive, higher rates.
- Vehicle types: pickups, box trucks, service vehicles — good appetite.
- Heavy trucks (Class 8): limited appetite, may need specialty carrier.
- New ventures: Progressive will write with higher rates and less
  experience credit.
- Prior carrier: preferred but not required.
- Loss history: 3-year lookback; more than 2 at-fault losses may
  decline.

## Radius rules

- **Local** — up to 50 miles from garaging address. Lowest rates.
- **Intermediate** — 50 to 200 miles. Moderate rates.
- **Long haul** — over 200 miles, often interstate. Highest rates,
  may require specialty carrier.
- Radius is determined by the farthest point the vehicle typically
  travels, not occasional trips.
- Misrepresenting radius to get a lower rate is a coverage risk — always
  use the actual operating radius.

## Garaging rules

- Garaging address is where the vehicle is parked at night, not the
  business address.
- If vehicles are parked at driver homes, each vehicle may have a
  different garaging address.
- Garaging ZIP code affects the rate (urban vs rural).
- If a vehicle is used for commute + business, the garaging address is
  still where it parks at night.

## DOT/MC lookup process

### DOT number
- Required for interstate commerce and some intrastate operations.
- Look up at: https://safer.fmcsa.dot.gov/CompanySnapshot.aspx
- Search by DOT number, MC number, or company name.
- Check: USDOT status (active), inspection records, crash data, safety
  rating.
- If no DOT number, the business may be too small to require one — ask
  about operations to confirm.

### MC number
- Required for interstate for-hire carriers.
- Look up at the same FMCSA site.
- Check: MC status (active), authority type (common, contract),
  insurance filings on file.
- If the business is interstate and for-hire but has no MC number, they
  may be operating without authority — flag to Lamar.

## Hired and non-owned auto explanation

**Hired auto** — covers vehicles the business rents, leases, or borrows
for business use. This is liability coverage (not physical damage to
the rented vehicle).

**Non-owned auto** — covers liability when an employee uses their
personal vehicle for business errands. It protects the business, not
the employee (the employee's personal insurance is primary).

**When to recommend:**
- Any business with employees who drive for work (even occasionally).
- Any business that rents vehicles.
- It is inexpensive and closes a significant gap.

**How to explain to the client:**
"If your employee runs to the bank in their own car and gets in an
accident, your business can be sued. Hired and non-owned auto covers
that. It also covers vehicles you rent for business. It is one of the
best values in commercial insurance."

## Filings checklist

- **Form E** — proof of financial responsibility (liability). Required
  for interstate carriers.
- **Form H** — proof of cargo insurance. Required for interstate
  for-hire carriers hauling freight.
- **Form E/H filings** are made by the insurance carrier to the FMCSA.
- **State filings** — some states require intrastate filings (check
  state requirements).
- If filings are required, the carrier must file them — Hermes/Gretchen
  cannot file directly.
- Track filing status: are they active? When were they last updated?
- If a filing lapses, the authority may be revoked — flag immediately.
