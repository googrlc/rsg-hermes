# Personal Lines Reference

The reference guide for personal lines quoting and service. This
complements `personal-lines-intake` (which extracts data from
documents) with the checklists, carrier rules, and coverage
explanations Gretchen needs day to day.

## When to use

- "What do I need for an auto quote?"
- "What are Progressive's rules for prior insurance?"
- "How do I explain roof age to a client?"
- "What does umbrella cover?"
- "What are the household driver rules?"

## Auto quote checklist

1. Named insured (full legal name)
2. Date of birth and license number
3. License status (valid, suspended, etc.)
4. Current address
5. All household drivers (name, DOB, license)
6. Vehicles (year, make, model, VIN, garaging address)
7. Current carrier
8. Current premium
9. Expiration date
10. Prior insurance (carrier, lapse if any)
11. Claims/accidents in last 5 years (date, type, amount)
12. Tickets in last 3 years (date, type)
13. Desired coverages (BI, PD, UM, MedPay, Comp, Coll, deductibles)
14. Any SR-22 or SR-22A requirement

## Home quote checklist

1. Named insured
2. Property address
3. Year built
4. Construction type (frame, brick, masonry)
5. Square footage
6. Roof type and age (last replaced)
7. Number of stories
8. Basement (yes/no, finished/unfinished)
9. Heating type and age
10. Electrical type and age (updated?)
11. Plumbing type and age
12. Security system (burglar, fire, monitored)
13. Current carrier
14. Current premium
15. Expiration date
16. Prior claims (last 5 years)
17. Mortgagee (lender name, loan number)
18. Desired coverages (Dwelling, Other Structures, Contents, Liability,
    deductible)

## Umbrella checklist

1. All underlying policies (auto, home, etc.) in force
2. Underlying limits confirmed (typically 250/500 or 300 CSL auto,
   $300K home liability)
3. Number of properties owned
4. Number of vehicles owned
5. Any youthful drivers in household
6. Any recreational vehicles (boat, RV, ATV)
7. Any rental properties
8. Desired umbrella limit ($1M, $2M, $3M, $5M)
9. Any prior umbrella claims

## Progressive personal auto rules

- Prior insurance preferred (6 months continuous coverage).
- No prior insurance: higher rate, may require higher down payment.
- SR-22: accepted, surcharge applies.
- Younger drivers: accepted with surcharge; good student discount
  available (3.0 GPA).
- Snapshot program available for usage-based discount.
- Multi-car discount (2+ vehicles).
- Home + auto bundle available through Progressive Home (formerly ASI).

## Safeco guidelines

- Preferred carrier — looks for good loss history.
- Prior insurance required for best rates.
- Roof age: 20 years max for replacement cost; over 20 years may get
  actual cash value only.
- Home + auto bundle: significant discount (Safeco package).
- Claim-free discount available.
- Protective devices discount (burglar + fire alarm).

## Home underwriting notes

- Roof age is the #1 underwriting concern. Over 15-20 years, carriers
  may decline or offer ACV only.
- Knob and tube or aluminum wiring: most carriers decline or require
  replacement.
- Galvanized plumbing: many carriers decline or require replacement.
- Wood stove: some carriers decline; others require inspection.
- Pool: most carriers accept with fence requirement; diving boards may
  be excluded.
- Trampoline: some carriers decline or require net enclosure.
- Dogs: breed restrictions vary by carrier (pit bull, Rottweiler,
  etc. may be declined).

## Prior insurance rules

- Most preferred carriers want 6 months continuous coverage.
- A lapse of even 1 day can move a client from preferred to standard
  rates.
- If no prior insurance, options: Progressive, SafeCo (sometimes),
  non-standard carriers.
- Always ask the reason for any lapse — a move, a sale, or a switch
  is treated differently than a cancellation for nonpayment.

## Household driver rules

- All licensed household members must be listed or excluded.
- Excluded driver: signed exclusion form required. Excluded driver has
  no coverage if they drive.
- Unlicensed household member (e.g., teenager with permit): list as
  permitted driver.
- College student away at school: may qualify for away-at-school
  discount (typically 100+ miles).
- Non-relative household member: some carriers require listing, some
  do not.

## Coverage explanation templates

### Liability (BI/PD)
"This pays if you are at fault in an accident and someone is hurt or
their property is damaged. The limits are split — for example,
250/500/100 means up to $250K per injured person, $500K total per
accident, and $100K for property damage."

### Comprehensive
"This covers damage to your vehicle that is not from a collision —
like theft, vandalism, hail, flooding, or hitting an animal. It has a
deductible you choose."

### Collision
"This covers damage to your vehicle from a collision — with another
car, a pole, a fence, etc. It also has a deductible."

### Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist
"This protects you if someone hits you and they do not have insurance
— or not enough. Your policy steps in to cover your injuries and
damages."

### Medical Payments
"This covers medical bills for you and your passengers regardless of
who is at fault. It is a small limit — usually $1,000 to $5,000 — and
it pays quickly."

### Umbrella
"This is an extra layer of liability protection above your auto and
home limits. If you are sued for more than your policy limits, the
umbrella kicks in. It typically starts at $1 million."
